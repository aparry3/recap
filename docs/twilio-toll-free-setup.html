Toll-free number & messaging setup

Everything to buy the number, pass toll-free verification on the first try, configure the Messaging Service, and turn on guest texting — with copy-paste values for every form field.

Prepared July 23, 2026 · ourweddingrecap.com · Values below were pulled from the live codebase (privacy, terms, /sms-consent, and the SMS code), so they match what a Twilio reviewer will actually see.

⚠ One thing to settle before anything else: the exact legal name

Your website's Terms, Privacy Policy, join-page footer, and /sms-consent page all say “Parry Technology and Media, LLC.” Your note to me said “Perry Technology Media, LLC.” These must match — and the one you enter in Twilio must match your state formation documents / IRS records exactly (spelling, “and,” comma, “LLC”). This guide uses Parry Technology and Media, LLC everywhere because that's what the site says. If the registered name is actually different, fix the site first, then use the registered spelling in Twilio. A legal-name/website mismatch is a classic verification rejection.

How this fits together

Three Twilio pieces, in the order you'll touch them:

  1. A toll-free number (8XX) — the number guests see and reply to. About $2–3/month.
  2. Toll-free verification (TFV) — a one-time compliance review of your brand, use case, and opt-in flow. For toll-free numbers this replaces the “A2P 10DLC campaign” you may have read about — there is no campaign registration, no EIN-based brand vetting, and no monthly campaign fees. TFV is the whole “campaign” step. Until it's approved, US/Canada texts from the number are blocked (error 30032), so we submit this early and do everything else while it's in review.
  3. A Messaging Service — the container that holds the number, receives inbound messages via webhook, and handles STOP/HELP. The app already expects one: it sends via TWILIO_MESSAGING_SERVICE_SID (src/lib/sms.ts).
You're in better shape than most applicants

The site already has everything reviewers ask for: a dedicated ourweddingrecap.com/sms-consent program page, SMS sections in the Privacy Policy (§5, including the required “we do not share mobile information…” clause) and Terms (§6), an unchecked consent checkbox with full CTIA language on the join form, a software-enforced cap on automated texts per guest per gallery, and STOP/START/HELP handling. The copy-paste answers below just point the reviewer at all of it.

2

Buy the toll-free number

  1. Console → Phone Numbers → Manage → Buy a number (or console.twilio.com/…/manage/search).
  2. Country United States, and switch the number-type filter to Toll-free.
  3. Check capabilities: the number must show SMS and MMS (guests text photos/videos to it — MMS is load-bearing, not optional). Voice doesn't matter.
  4. Any 8XX prefix works (833/844/855/866/877/888). If you want something memorable, use the search box to look for digit patterns.
  5. Buy it (~$2–3/month, shown before purchase) and note the number in E.164 form, e.g. +18885551234.
Skip the number's own webhook config

Don't set messaging webhooks on the number itself. In step 5 the number joins a Messaging Service, and the service's inbound settings take over.

5

Create the Messaging Service (while verification is in review)

  1. Console → Messaging → Services → Create Messaging Service.
  2. Step 1 — name & use case:
    Messaging Service friendly name
    Our Wedding Recap
    Carriers can surface this name; keep it the brand. For “What do you want to use messaging for?” choose Notify my users.
  3. Step 2 — sender pool: Add sender → Phone number → select your new toll-free number.
  4. Step 3 — integration (inbound messages): select Send a webhook.
    Request URL (HTTP POST)
    https://ourweddingrecap.com/api/webhooks/twilio/inbound

    The host must match the app's BASE_URL env var character-for-character (scheme, www or no www). The route rebuilds this URL from BASE_URL to check Twilio's signature — a mismatch means every inbound message 401s. Check what production uses (Vercel → Settings → Environment Variables, or vercel env ls) before pasting.

    Leave the fallback URL and the service-level “Callback URL” for status empty — the app attaches its own status callback (/api/webhooks/twilio/status) to each outbound message.

  5. Step 4 — compliance: it will point you at the toll-free verification you already submitted. Finish setup.

Service settings to confirm (service → Properties / Integration / Opt-out management)

  • Opt-out management: enabled (default). Twilio auto-answers STOP/HELP and still forwards the message to the webhook, where the app records the suppression — you want both layers.
  • Smart encoding: on — silently converts curly quotes and other non-GSM characters couples paste in, which keeps messages at 153 chars/segment instead of 67.
  • Sticky sender: on (default) — irrelevant with one number, harmless later.
  • Copy the Messaging Service SID (starts with MG) from the service's Properties page — you need it in step 6.

Optional: custom STOP / HELP / START replies

Under Opt-out management → Advanced opt-out, replace Twilio's generic auto-replies with branded ones. Keep the keyword lists at their defaults (they already match what the app recognizes: STOP, STOPALL, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, END, QUIT, REVOKE, OPTOUT / START, YES, UNSTOP / HELP, INFO).

Opt-out (STOP) reply
Our Wedding Recap: You're unsubscribed and will receive no more messages from this number. Reply START to rejoin. Help: aaron@ourweddingrecap.com

8

Message templates, workshopped

How the plumbing shapes the writing — three things the app adds to every reminder automatically:

  • The prefix “Our Wedding Recap: ” — so never write “powered by Our Wedding Recap” in a body; it would appear twice.
  • The footer with the gallery link and compliance line: “View & upload: [gallery link] / Or reply here with photos/videos to add them. Reply STOP to stop, HELP for help.” — so never put “click here” or a URL in a body, and don't restate STOP.
  • A hard cap of 10 automated texts per guest per gallery (confirmation included) — a full program below uses 5–6, leaving headroom.

Writing rules that keep costs down and delivery clean: plain apostrophes and hyphens, no emoji and no curly “smart” quotes (one non-GSM character switches the whole message to 67-char segments — roughly double the cost across 150 guests), and speak in the couple's voice (“we,” “our”) since guests experience these as coming from the couple. Each template below is the body to paste into the Recap reminder composer; expand it to see exactly what the guest's phone will show.

9

Costs, timeline, and if verification bounces

What it costs (ballpark — confirm at twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/us)

  • Number: ~$2–3/month.
  • Outbound SMS: ~$0.008 per segment + ~$0.003 toll-free carrier fee. A 150-guest wedding running the full 6-message program is roughly 2,500–3,000 segments ≈ $30–40 per wedding — worth knowing for pricing galleries later.
  • Inbound SMS/MMS (guests texting photos): a cent or two each; negligible.
  • Toll-free verification itself: free. No 10DLC brand/campaign fees ever, on this path.

Timeline & states

  • Review typically lands in 1–5 business days; allow two weeks of buffer before the first real wedding. Status flows In Review → Verified (or Rejected/Update Required) on the Compliance page, with email updates.
  • Until Verified, US and Canada messages from the number are blocked outright (error 30032) — don't schedule anything before approval.
  • Verified toll-free throughput is plenty for this use (hundreds of guests dispatch in well under a minute; the app batches sends 10 at a time anyway).
  • Coverage is US + Canada. The app only enrolls US numbers today (it normalizes to US format), so international guests simply won't get texts — the gallery link via email covers them.

If it's rejected

Rejections name a reason; you edit the same submission and resubmit. The usual suspects, pre-answered:

Rejection reasonFix
Opt-in evidence insufficient / unclearRetake the join-form screenshot so the entire disclosure paragraph is sharp and readable in one frame; confirm the submitted evidence URLs open in incognito (no auth wall).
Website doesn't match business namePoint to the footer line “owned and operated by Parry Technology and Media, LLC” (on the create page, Terms, Privacy, and /sms-consent) — and restate the DBA sentence from Additional Information.
Use case too vague / looks like marketingResubmit emphasizing: self-enrolled event guests only, low-volume software-capped updates, no promotional content, opt-in documented at /sms-consent.
Privacy policy missing SMS languageAlready covered (§5 has the no-sharing clause) — reply citing https://ourweddingrecap.com/privacy section 5 verbatim.
Sample messages missing brand or STOPUse the three samples from step 4 unmodified — every one carries both.

If a rejection cites anything else, the reason code arrives by email — search it on Twilio's “Toll-Free Verification rejection reasons” support page, fix, resubmit. Second-round reviews are usually fast.

Appendix: small nits noticed along the way

  • “Wedding wedding.” The confirmation reads “…automated [Gallery Name] wedding updates.” A gallery named “Smith & Johnson Wedding” produces “automated Smith & Johnson Wedding wedding updates.” Either steer couples toward gallery names without a trailing “Wedding,” or drop the word “wedding” from the confirmation copy in src/lib/reminders/dispatch.ts.
  • Volume estimate drift. If bookings grow past ~10 weddings/month, edit the verification's volume estimate upward — cheap insurance against carrier filtering at the new scale.
  • Keep the evidence fresh. If the join form's consent wording ever changes, retake and redeploy the screenshots in public/compliance/ — stale evidence is a headache during any future re-review.
  • Don't delete the number. Verification attaches to the specific number. Releasing it means re-verifying a new one from scratch.
